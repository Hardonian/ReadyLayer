# ReadyLayer Packaging and Pricing Boundary

## Open source

ReadyLayer's repository is Apache-2.0 licensed. Self-hosted users operate their own infrastructure, credentials, upgrades,
backups, and support process. Infrastructure and third-party model costs are paid directly by the operator.

## Enterprise design-partner pilot

Enterprise work is currently scoped as a design-partner engagement rather than a public self-serve plan. Pricing is based
on the work and risk boundary in an approved proposal, including:

- repository and provider scope;
- deployment topology and data residency;
- onboarding and policy-mapping work;
- support and escalation coverage;
- evidence retention and export requirements;
- custom integration or assurance work.

There is no repository-defined public price, automatic trial, discount, refund policy, service level, or included support
window. Those terms are commitments only when present in an executed agreement.

## Packaging principles

1. **OSS remains useful on its own.** Core governance logic is not weakened to force a hosted upgrade.
2. **Charge for operational responsibility.** Managed infrastructure, support, assurance work, and integration effort are
   the commercial value—not a hidden policy algorithm.
3. **Price after scope.** Do not quote before understanding deployment, data, provider, and control requirements.
4. **Make exit possible.** Evidence and configuration exports are part of the evaluation plan.
5. **Avoid surprise usage exposure.** Any metered cost must define its unit, measurement source, alert thresholds, and cap.

## Proposal checklist

- [ ] Named buyer, technical champion, and accountable security owner
- [ ] In-scope repositories and protected change classes
- [ ] Success and stop criteria
- [ ] Deployment and data-flow diagram
- [ ] Support and incident responsibilities
- [ ] Third-party costs and usage assumptions
- [ ] Commercial terms and renewal mechanics
- [ ] Export and offboarding plan
